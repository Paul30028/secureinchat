Mizpah · encrypted group messenger

A relay that forwards.
Devices that hold the keys.

SecureInChat messaging is a private noticeboard and group chat for small circles. The relay only forwards ciphertext it cannot decrypt. It is still being built.

The client apps are not released yet, and there has been no independent third-party security audit. The production relay can be tested from your browser today.

03Threat model

What it protects, from whom, and what it cannot hide.

The apps are in development and have not been independently audited, so this describes the design, not verified behaviour. Last updated 2026-11-05.

Designed to protect

  • Message and file content. Keys stay on the devices, and the relay only forwards encrypted payloads it cannot decrypt.
  • Group names. In the design a group's display name exists only on each device and is never sent to the relay.
  • Announcements from the group admin. They carry a signature from the creator's key, and every client rejects announcements it cannot verify.

Designed to resist

  • Someone who breaches the relay, or compels its operator to hand over data. The relay has no message content and is designed without persistent storage of message content.
  • Someone watching the network between a device and the relay. Connections use TLS, and the payload inside is encrypted again end to end.

How a group key reaches members

There is no public-key exchange between members. When a group is created, the app generates a random group key and puts it inside the invite code (the link or QR code). Whoever has a valid invite code can join and read the group's messages.

Treat an invite like a house key: share it only in person or through a channel you trust.

What the relay can still see

A relay that routes messages cannot avoid some metadata. In the current design it sees:

Your network addressThe relay uses it only to limit repeated connection attempts and keeps a salted hash rather than the address itself; the hosting provider and any network or tunnel in front of the relay (currently Cloudflare) can still see it.
When devices connectWhen devices connect and when messages arrive.
Payload sizeThe size of each payload, which can hint at its type.
Routing identifiersThe identifiers needed to route messages, such as which group a device joins.

Not keeping connection history is a design goal. Removing this metadata entirely is not realistic for a server that routes messages.

What it does not protect against

These are the honest boundaries of the design. Every item here is a place where privacy depends on you.

A compromised phoneMalware, or someone holding your unlocked device, can read what you can read.
People in the groupAnyone who receives a message can copy it, screenshot it or forward it.
A leaked invite codeAnyone who gets it can read the group until the admin changes the key and sends a new invite only to the people who should stay.
A key that leaks laterEach group key is used for every message until it is changed, so there is no forward secrecy within that period: if the key leaks, recorded messages from that period can be decrypted.
Removed members keeping old messagesRemoval moves the group to new keys, so later messages are unreadable to them, but messages already delivered stay on their device.
Losing the admin keyAdmin rights belong to whoever holds the creator's signing key, so losing it needs a recovery step.

How it compares

Group key changes on removal are weaker than the continuous key ratcheting that the Signal protocol or MLS provide. We do not claim equivalent security.

04Building blocks

The cryptographic primitives named in the design.

These describe the design, not an independent audit or a legal certification.

ECDSA P-256

Signatures for device sign-in to the relay and admin announcements. The group key itself travels inside the invite code.

AES-256-GCM

Authenticated encryption intended to protect confidentiality and integrity of message payloads.

HKDF

Context-separated derivation of session and message keys.

These name the primitives in the design. They are not a certification. More detail: what a blind relay can and cannot know. You can check the live relay connection from your browser.
