Protect your phone number: SIM PIN, carrier locks and the SIM-swap scam

Your phone number is often a recovery key for important accounts. These steps make it harder for someone to take it over—and explain what each protection cannot do.

By Paul Updated 22 October 2026 8 minute read
  • Sources linked
  • Carrier controls vary

1. Put a PIN on the SIM itself

A SIM PIN protects the SIM if someone removes it from a phone or restarts a stolen device. It does not prevent a carrier-level SIM swap; that requires protection from the carrier.

On iPhone, look under Cellular settings for SIM PIN. On Android, search Settings for SIM lock; the exact menu varies by manufacturer. Before enabling it, confirm the default PIN with your carrier. Do not guess: too many incorrect attempts can lock the SIM and require a PUK code.

Before you start

Do not guess a SIM PIN or PUK. Confirm the correct code with your carrier first and store any recovery information safely.

2. Set an account PIN with your carrier

This is a separate code a carrier should ask for before changing account details. Do not reuse your SIM PIN, bank PIN or date of birth. Check the provider's own instructions, because requirements and menu names vary.

3. Turn on available number or SIM-change protection

Some carriers offer a lock that blocks a SIM change, a transfer to another carrier, or both. Availability and names vary by provider and country. Ask the carrier what the lock blocks, how a change request is confirmed, and how to receive a notice if someone requests one.

4. Stop relying only on SMS codes for important accounts

A SIM swap can redirect text messages and calls. For important accounts—especially your email, bank, password manager and primary identity account—consider an authenticator app or security key when the provider supports it. Review recovery methods as well as sign-in methods.

5. Keep your number out of public profiles

A public phone number can help scammers target you or answer weak identity-verification questions. Review social profiles, marketplace listings, public bios and old posts. Remove the number where it is not needed, and avoid making it visible to everyone by default.

6. Review phone-number and registration settings in messengers

In Signal, review phone-number visibility and “who can find me by my number” settings, and consider Registration Lock. Other services have different controls; use their current official instructions rather than assuming the same setting exists everywhere.

If you think your number has been taken over

Contact the carrier through a number or website you already trust and ask it to restore control of the number. Then change passwords on important accounts, review recovery details and check financial activity for anything you did not authorise.

What we have not tested

This guide explains steps based on provider and regulator documentation. We have not tested every carrier's lock or how quickly a carrier sends an alert. Menu labels can change, and the protections available to you depend on your carrier and region.
