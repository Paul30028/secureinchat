Phone number privacy checkup

Review six protective measures. Checking an item means you reviewed it or took an appropriate action; this page does not verify your device or account settings.

No phone number needed

Do not enter your phone number here. Checkbox state exists only in this page’s memory; it is not saved or sent. Refreshing or leaving the page clears it.

Work through the checklist

This is a self-review checklist. It does not scan your phone settings or verify whether an account has a protection enabled.

01

Why it matters A SIM PIN can add a layer of protection when a SIM is used in another device or after a restart. Device and carrier behaviour varies, and too many incorrect attempts can lock the SIM.

What to do Open your device’s SIM or mobile-network security settings. If you plan to enable a PIN, make sure you understand the recovery process and know how to obtain the PUK from your carrier before entering guesses.

Official source: Apple: Use a SIM PIN for your iPhone · Google: Pixel SIM lock

02

Why it matters An attacker who convinces a carrier to transfer or replace your SIM may receive calls or SMS intended for you.

What to do Contact your mobile carrier using its official app, account portal or published support number. Ask about account PINs, port-out locks, number locks and SIM-swap protection; enable the options available for your plan.

Official source: Verizon · T-Mobile · AT&T Wireless Account Lock · Ofcom (UK)

03

Why it matters SMS codes depend on continued control of your phone number and can be exposed to number-transfer attacks. Stronger options may be available for important accounts.

What to do Prioritise email, financial and primary identity accounts. Where supported, consider passkeys, an authenticator app or a security key, and store recovery codes securely.

Official source: FTC: Use two-factor authentication · CISA: More than a password

04

Why it matters A publicly visible phone number can make it easier to connect different profiles to the same person and may increase unwanted calls or messages.

What to do Review social profiles, public bios, marketplace listings and old posts. Remove the number from public-facing fields where possible and check who can see account contact details.

Official source: X: phone discoverability · LinkedIn: profile visibility

05

Why it matters Some data broker services publish or sell contact details alongside names, locations or inferred relationships. Coverage and opt-out processes vary.

What to do Read the opt-out guide before submitting information to a broker site. Use the site’s published process, verify the domain carefully and avoid unfamiliar services that ask for unnecessary personal data.

Read the data broker opt-out guide →

Official source: Our opt-out guide links each broker's official page

06

Why it matters A registration lock or app-specific PIN can add protection against another person attempting to register an account with your number. The exact feature differs by app.

What to do Open the security or account settings of each messaging app you use. Check its official instructions for registration lock, account PIN or equivalent protection and enable it if appropriate.

Official source: Signal: PIN and registration lock · WhatsApp: two-step verification
