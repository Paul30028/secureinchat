How an IPv6 leak happens

Most connections today have both an IPv4 and an IPv6 address. Many VPN clients create a tunnel for IPv4 only. When a website offers IPv6, your system prefers it, and that traffic leaves through your normal connection. The site sees your real network while the VPN shows a different IPv4 address.

This test asks for both addresses, finds which network owns each, and compares them. Matching networks mean both routes go through the same exit.

How to close a leak

  • Turn on IPv6 leak protection in your VPN client, or pick a VPN that tunnels IPv6.
  • Use the client's kill switch so traffic outside the tunnel is blocked.
  • If neither is possible, disable IPv6 on your network adapter or router. This removes a working connection, so reverse it if you stop using the VPN.
  • Run this test again afterwards, and the WebRTC leak test too.

Frequently asked questions

What is an IPv6 leak?

Your device has two internet addresses, IPv4 and IPv6. If a VPN tunnels only IPv4, sites that support IPv6 reach you directly on the IPv6 address, outside the tunnel, and see your real network.

How does this test decide?

It fetches your IPv4 and IPv6 addresses separately, looks up which network owns each, and compares them. Different networks with the VPN box ticked is reported as a leak. A failed lookup is reported as a warning, not a pass.

My result says no IPv6 connection. Is that good?

For leak purposes, yes: with no IPv6 route there is nothing to leak. It only means your network or browser did not reach the internet over IPv6 during the test.

Should I disable IPv6?

Disabling it on your device closes the leak, but it also removes a working connection. A VPN that tunnels or blocks IPv6, a setting often called IPv6 leak protection, is the cleaner fix.

Is the network name always right?

Usually, but it comes from IP data that can be out of date, and two networks of one provider can look different. Treat a mismatch as a reason to check, not as proof.

Does this page store my address?

The page's code stores nothing and sets no cookies. The lookups it makes, to ipify and ipwho.is, are listed on the IP leak test page under How the test works.
