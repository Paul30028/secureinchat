Method and limits

What this test reads

Any page you visit can read the signals below without asking. This test reads them and tells you which ones help a tracker recognise you, and which defences your browser already applies.

  • Canvas: draws an image twice. A stable result is a signal. A different result each time suggests your browser or an extension adds noise on purpose.
  • Graphics card: the GPU name WebGL can report. Some browsers hide it.
  • Global Privacy Control and Do Not Track: whether your browser sends opt-out signals.
  • Browser and device: user agent, languages, time zone, screen and hardware details.

What this test cannot tell you

It does not test fonts, audio processing or every API, and it cannot say how rare your combination is. Treat the result as a list of what your browser exposes, and re-run it after you change browser, settings or extensions. For your network address, run the IP leak test.

Frequently asked questions

What is browser fingerprinting?

Sites read many small details from your browser, such as screen size, fonts, graphics output and language, and combine them into a value that recognises you without cookies.

Does this test tell me how unique I am?

No. A real uniqueness score needs a large dataset of other browsers, which this page does not have. It shows what your browser exposes and which defences are active.

What does a varying canvas result mean?

The page draws the same image twice. If the results differ, your browser or an extension appears to add noise on purpose, which would break the use of canvas as a stable identifier. Brave and some Firefox settings do this. The test sees the difference but cannot confirm the method.

Is a stable canvas hash bad?

It is normal in most browsers. It matters because trackers combine it with other signals. Compare the hash across browsers on one device to see how much of it is yours alone.

What is Global Privacy Control?

A browser signal that asks sites not to sell or share your data. Some jurisdictions require sites to treat it as an opt-out. It does not hide anything from the site itself.

Does Do Not Track help?

Rarely. Most sites ignore it, and because few people turn it on, setting it can be one more detail that tells you apart.

How can I reduce fingerprinting?

Use a browser that randomizes or blocks the high-value signals, keep it updated, avoid unusual extensions and settings, and do not resize the window to odd values. Run the test again after any change.

Does this page store my data?

No. Everything is computed in your browser and nothing is sent. The page sets no cookies and loads no analytics.
