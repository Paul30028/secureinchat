Phone number privacy checkup

Review six protective measures. Checking an item means you reviewed it or took an appropriate action; this page does not verify your device or account settings.

No phone number needed

Do not enter your phone number here. Checkbox state exists only in this page’s memory; it is not saved or sent. Refreshing or leaving the page clears it.

Work through the checklist

This is a self-review checklist. It does not scan your phone settings or verify whether an account has a protection enabled.

0 of 6
01

Why it matters A SIM PIN can add a layer of protection when a SIM is used in another device or after a restart. Device and carrier behaviour varies, and too many incorrect attempts can lock the SIM.

What to do Open your device’s SIM or mobile-network security settings. If you plan to enable a PIN, make sure you understand the recovery process and know how to obtain the PUK from your carrier before entering guesses.

Official source: Apple: Use a SIM PIN for your iPhone · Google: Pixel SIM lock

02

Why it matters An attacker who convinces a carrier to transfer or replace your SIM may receive calls or SMS intended for you.

What to do Contact your mobile carrier using its official app, account portal or published support number. Ask about account PINs, port-out locks, number locks and SIM-swap protection; enable the options available for your plan. See the carrier table below.

Official source: Verizon · T-Mobile · AT&T Wireless Account Lock · Ofcom (UK)

03

Why it matters SMS codes depend on continued control of your phone number and can be exposed to number-transfer attacks. Stronger options may be available for important accounts.

What to do Prioritise email, financial and primary identity accounts. Where supported, consider passkeys, an authenticator app or a security key, and store recovery codes securely.

Official source: FTC: Use two-factor authentication · CISA: More than a password

04

Why it matters A publicly visible phone number can make it easier to connect different profiles to the same person and may increase unwanted calls or messages.

What to do Review social profiles, public bios, marketplace listings and old posts. Remove the number from public-facing fields where possible and check who can see account contact details.

Official source: X: phone discoverability · LinkedIn: profile visibility

05

Why it matters Some data broker services publish or sell contact details alongside names, locations or inferred relationships. Coverage and opt-out processes vary.

What to do Read the opt-out guide before submitting information to a broker site. Use the site’s published process, verify the domain carefully and avoid unfamiliar services that ask for unnecessary personal data.

Read the data broker opt-out guide →

Official source: Our opt-out guide links each broker's official page

06

Why it matters A registration lock or app-specific PIN can add protection against another person attempting to register an account with your number. The exact feature differs by app.

What to do Open the security or account settings of each messaging app you use. Check its official instructions for registration lock, account PIN or equivalent protection and enable it if appropriate.

Official source: Signal: PIN and registration lock · WhatsApp: two-step verification

Carrier number locks and transfer PINs

What each carrier’s own pages say about locking your number and about the PIN another carrier needs to move it. Every row links to the official page it comes from. A lock helps only if the account owner turns it on; turn it off before you move your number yourself.

CarrierLock and what it blocksHow to turn it onTransfer PIN (when you leave)
Verizon (US) Number Lock stops a Number Transfer PIN being created, so the number cannot be ported. SIM Protection blocks SIM swaps, upgrades and bring-your-own-device activations on protected lines. After you turn SIM Protection off, SIM changes stay blocked for at least 15 minutes. My Verizon app: Me → Edit profile and settings → Security → SIM Protection or Number Lock, then switch on each number. Account Owner or Account Manager on a standard monthly account: My Verizon, or dial #PORT from the phone. Expires after 7 days. Number Lock must be off first.
T-Mobile (US) SIM Protection (free on postpaid) stops your number being moved to another device. Port Out Protection blocks transfers to another carrier and is added line by line. An account PIN/passcode (6–15 digits) is used to verify you when you contact customer service. T-Life: Manage → gear icon → Security → SIM Protection. Port Out Protection: Manage → See Plans → Manage add-ons. Authorised users can add both; on postpaid, only the Primary Account Holder can remove them. Only the Primary Account Holder: T-Life on the T-Mobile network with Wi-Fi off → Account → gear icon → Permission & controls → Transfer PIN. Port Out Protection must be off. The page does not say how long the PIN lasts.
AT&T (US) Wireless Account Lock (free, introduced July 2025) blocks number transfers, SIM swaps, device purchases and billing changes. Business accounts have a separate Business Account Lock; AT&T says prepaid has a similar lock. Postpaid consumer accounts: toggle in the myAT&T app. The account owner and people given secondary online access can manage it. Primary online access users: account profile → Settings → Transfer phone number → Request new PIN, or dial *PORT (needs your account passcode). Expires after 4 days (14 for business). The lock must be off.
Google Fi (US) Number Lock (no extra cost) stops your number being moved to another phone or ported to another carrier. Fi website: Phone settings → Privacy & security → Number lock, sign in again, switch on. Google warns that if you lose access to your Google Account with Number Lock on, Fi cannot give you your port-out details, so add a recovery email or recovery contact first. Fi app or website: Account → Manage plan → leave Google Fi → Transfer your number to another carrier → Get Transfer Info. Expires if unused for 14 days.
UK (all networks) We did not find an official EE, Vodafone, O2 or Three page describing a port-out lock, so none is listed. Ask your network directly. — Ofcom’s text-to-switch: text PAC to 65075 from your phone. The code is valid for 30 days; the new provider should complete the switch within one working day.
