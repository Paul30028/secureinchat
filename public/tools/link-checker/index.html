What it checks

Tracking parameters such as utm_source, fbclid and gclid; text before an @ that hides the real site; raw IP addresses; international characters that imitate a familiar name (shown decoded); unencrypted http; unusual ports; link shorteners that hide the destination; a well-known brand name on someone else's site; long chains of subdomains; and a second address hidden inside the link.

What it does not do

It does not open the link, look it up in a phishing database or check the page content. A brand-new scam site with an ordinary name will show no warning signs. The brand check covers a short list of companies often imitated, and only whole words. The safest habit stays the same: for anything involving a login or a payment, open the service from your own bookmark or app instead of the link.

How your link is handled

The link is processed in this page. It is not sent to us or anyone else, and the page loads no analytics. Related: redact text before you share it, IP leak test.

Frequently asked questions

Is the link opened or sent anywhere?

No. The checks run in this page. The link is not opened, fetched, sent to us or to a reputation service, and nothing is stored.

Does "no warning signs" mean the link is safe?

No. These are local checks for common tricks. A new phishing site on an ordinary-looking name will pass. Check that the site name is exactly the one you expect, and when in doubt open the service from your own bookmark instead of the link.

Does removing tracking parameters break the link?

Usually not. The tool only removes parameters known to be used for tracking, such as utm_source or fbclid. If a page stops working, use the original link.

Why not check the link against a phishing database?

A database lookup would send your link to a third party, and links can contain personal tokens. We may add it as an option you have to switch on, with the service named on the page.
