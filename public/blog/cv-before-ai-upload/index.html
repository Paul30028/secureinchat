Before you paste your CV into an AI tool: what to take out

A CV holds your name, contact details, addresses, dates and employer history. What to remove or generalise before you ask an AI assistant to improve it, and what automatic redaction can and cannot do.

By Paul2026-10-20

People paste CVs into AI assistants to get them rewritten or tailored. A CV is dense with personal data. We make no claim about what any provider does with it; check the current data-controls page of the service you use. Here is what you control.

What a CV usually contains

  • Full name, email, phone number, home address and links to profiles.
  • Employers, job titles and dates, which together can identify you even without your name.
  • Sometimes date of birth, nationality, photo, and references with their contact details.

What to take out first

  1. Run the text through our text redactor in your browser. It replaces emails and phone numbers with placeholders. Nothing is uploaded.
  2. Replace your name and address with [NAME] and [ADDRESS] yourself. A pattern tool does not recognise names or street addresses.
  3. Generalise identifying details if the task allows it: "a mid-size UK retailer" instead of the employer's name.
  4. Leave out references' contact details and anything you would not post publicly.

Put the details back afterwards

The redactor gives equal values the same placeholder, such as [EMAIL_1], so the improved text keeps its structure. When the assistant returns the text, put your real details back by hand.

Limits

Redaction finds formats, not meaning, so read the result. Rewriting a CV without personal data does not stop the content itself from being yours: unusual projects or employers can still point to you.
