Redact text before you share it: what to remove from logs, emails and configs

By Paul · 2026-10-20

People paste logs, emails and configuration files into bug reports, forums and AI assistants every day. Those texts often carry more than the problem: addresses, tokens and names. Once posted, they are hard to take back. This page lists what to look for and how our text redactor helps, including what it misses.

What hides in ordinary text

  • Network details: IPv4 and IPv6 addresses, MAC addresses and internal hostnames in logs and traces.
  • Contact details: email addresses and phone numbers in headers, signatures and error reports.
  • Secrets: API keys, access tokens, JSON Web Tokens, private keys and passwords inside connection strings such as postgres://user:password@host.
  • Payment data: card numbers in order exports and support tickets.

What the redactor does

It matches common patterns and replaces each with a labelled placeholder such as [EMAIL_1]. The same value always receives the same placeholder, so a log stays readable: you can still see that one address appeared five times without seeing the address. It runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded.

What it cannot find

  • Names, street addresses, company names and anything without a fixed format.
  • Secrets in a custom format that does not look like a known key.
  • Card numbers that fail the standard checksum. A made-up number is left alone on purpose, to avoid replacing ordinary long numbers.
  • Context that identifies you without a pattern, such as a unique error message from your own project.

That is why a tool like this reduces the risk but does not remove it. Read the result before you share it.

A short checklist

  1. Run the text through the redactor and read the output line by line.
  2. Search the result for names, paths that include your user name, and project or customer names.
  3. If a real secret was ever in the text, rotate it. Redacting a copy does not cancel a key that was already exposed.
  4. Share the smallest piece of text that shows the problem.
