Before you upload a bank statement to an AI chatbot: what to remove, and what our redactor misses

We ran our text redactor on a made-up bank statement. It caught the card, email and phone number, and missed the name, address and account number. What to remove by hand before you paste or upload financial text to an AI assistant.

By Paul2026-10-20

Many people ask an AI assistant to summarise a bank or card statement. Before you do, decide what the assistant needs to see. This page does not claim what any AI provider does with uploads: policies differ and change, so read the provider's current data-controls page. It covers the part you control, which is what you hand over.

What we tested

We wrote a fake statement and ran it through our text redactor, which works in your browser and uploads nothing. The made-up input had a name, a street address, an account number, a sort code, an IBAN, a card number, an email, a phone number and two transactions.

  • Found and replaced: the card number (after a checksum check), the email address and the phone number. After this test we also added IBAN detection, with the standard checksum, so a valid IBAN is now replaced too.
  • Not found: the account holder's name, the street address, the plain account number, the sort code, the employer in the payroll line and the names of people paid. These have no fixed pattern, so a pattern tool cannot know them.

That is the honest limit of any automatic redaction: it finds formats, not meaning. Read the result yourself.

Remove by hand before you share

  • Your name and address, and the names of people you pay or receive from.
  • Account numbers, sort codes, routing numbers and IBANs. Card numbers, even partial ones.
  • Reference numbers, customer IDs and anything that identifies the statement or the account.
  • Employer names in payroll lines, and unusual merchant names that point to where you live or work.

A safer way to ask

  1. Copy only the lines you need, not the whole document. A PDF cannot be pasted into our tool as a file; copy the text out of it.
  2. Run the text through the redactor, then read the output and replace names and addresses yourself, for example with [NAME] and [ADDRESS].
  3. Ask the question about the pattern, such as "which of these are subscriptions", rather than uploading the original.

What this does not do

It does not make an upload safe, and it does not remove information that can be worked out from context. If a statement is truly sensitive, think twice about sharing it at all. Related: redact text before you share it.
