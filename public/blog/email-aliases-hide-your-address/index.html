Email aliases: give every site its own address and switch off the ones that leak

An email alias forwards to your real inbox, so a shop or app never sees your address. How Apple Hide My Email and Firefox Relay work, what they cost, how to turn an alias off, and the accounts that should never use one.

By Paul2026-10-22

Your email address is the key that links your accounts together. Data brokers, leaked databases and marketing lists all use it. An alias is a separate address that forwards to your real inbox. Give each site its own alias, and when one starts receiving spam you know who leaked or sold it, and you can switch that alias off without changing anything else. Every fact below links to the provider's own documentation. No link on this page earns us anything.

Two services you may already have

Apple Hide My Email

  • Creates unique, random addresses. Requires iCloud+, or someone in your Family Sharing group with iCloud+.
  • You can send and reply from these addresses in Mail on iCloud.com.
  • To switch one off: sign in at icloud.com/icloudplus, select Hide My Email, choose the address, then Deactivate email address. Mail sent to a deactivated address is returned to the sender. You can reactivate it at any time from the inactive addresses at the bottom of the list.

Sources: Use Hide My Email, Stop using or reactivate addresses.

Firefox Relay

  • Calls its aliases email masks. Free users can create up to 50.
  • Create one from the dashboard at relay.firefox.com (Generate new mask), or in Firefox from the email field of a sign-up form.
  • Blocking promotional mail on a mask needs Relay Premium. Deleting masks frees up slots on the free plan.

Source: Create an email mask through Firefox Relay.

Where to use an alias, and where not to

Use an aliasUse your real address
Shops, newsletters, apps you are trying, forums, Wi-Fi sign-ins, loyalty schemesYour bank, tax and government accounts, your phone carrier, your password manager, and the recovery email of your main accounts

The reason for the right-hand column: an alias depends on the alias service and on your account with it. If either stops working, every account behind that alias stops receiving password resets. Keep the accounts you cannot afford to lose on an address you control directly. That is also why alias accounts deserve two-step verification; see the account checkup.

What an alias does not hide

  • The alias service sees who writes to you and, unless the mail is encrypted, what they write.
  • It does not hide your name, phone number, delivery address or payment card, which most shops still ask for.
  • Replying from your real inbox instead of through the alias can reveal your real address. Use the reply option the service provides.

For what happens to your address once it is out there, see what data brokers know about you. If you are also changing your main inbox, see moving to encrypted email. We have not yet tested reply handling or delivery for either service.
