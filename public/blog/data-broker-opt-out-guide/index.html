Data broker opt-out guide: the US, the UK and the EU, with official removal pages

By Paul · 2026-10-22

Data brokers collect personal details from public records, purchases and online activity and sell or license the result; what data brokers know about you explains how. This guide is the practical half: where to send a removal or opt-out request, region by region. Every step links to the page of the government body or company that runs it, checked in October 2026. Where we could not open a company's own page, we left that company out rather than guess its process.

Use these pages only for your own records, or for someone who has asked you to act for them. They are not a way to look people up.

Before you start

  • Use an email alias for confirmation emails, so the request does not hand a broker a new, verified address.
  • Keep a simple list: site, date, what you asked for, and the reply. Profiles can reappear, and the list tells you when to check again.
  • Give the minimum the form needs. Where a field is marked optional, you can leave it empty.

United States

There is no single national deletion law. What you can ask for depends on your state and on each company's own policy.

1. California residents: one request to every registered broker (DROP)

  • What it does: the Delete Request and Opt-out Platform, run by the California Privacy Protection Agency, sends one request to every broker on the state's registry, over 600 of them, asking each to delete your information and not sell it. It is free.
  • Who can use it: California residents. You confirm residency through the California Identity Gateway; the agency says your verification details are not kept by DROP.
  • Timing: requests have been open since January 1, 2026. Since August 1, 2026, registered brokers must check DROP at least once every 45 days. The agency says status updates can take up to 90 days to appear and results vary.
  • Limits: it reaches brokers on California's registry, not every company that holds your data.

Source: California Privacy Protection Agency: Delete Request and Opt-out Platform (DROP).

2. Prescreened credit and insurance offers

  • What it does: stops the national credit bureaus from putting you on lists for prescreened offers, for five years or permanently.
  • How: optoutprescreen.com or 1-888-567-8688, both run by the credit bureaus. A permanent opt-out needs a signed form you receive afterwards. The FTC notes that the Social Security number and date of birth fields are optional.

Source: FTC: How to stop junk mail.

3. Marketing mail (DMAchoice)

  • What it does: registration with DMAchoice, run by the Association of National Advertisers, lasts 10 years and stops most, not all, promotional mail.
  • Cost: the FTC lists a $6 fee online or $7 by mail. Weigh that against how much marketing mail you get.

Source: FTC: How to stop junk mail.

United Kingdom

5. Opt out of the open register

  • What it is: the electoral register has two versions. The open register is an extract that anyone can buy; the full register is used for elections and some checks, such as credit applications.
  • How: online through the GOV.UK register to vote service, even if you are already registered, or through your local electoral registration office. In Northern Ireland, contact the Electoral Office for Northern Ireland.
  • Cost to you: none. Opting out does not affect your right to vote. You cannot opt out of the full register.

Source: GOV.UK: Opt out of the open register.

6. Ask a company to stop marketing with your data, or to delete it

  • Direct marketing: you can object at any time, and the ICO says organisations cannot refuse an objection to direct marketing. They may keep your name on a suppression list so they do not contact you by mistake.
  • Deletion: the right to erasure applies in set cases, for example when the data is no longer needed for its original purpose, you withdraw consent, or it was collected unlawfully. It is not absolute; an organisation can refuse when an exemption applies, but must tell you why.
  • How: write to the company, say which data and which use you object to. No special wording is needed; the ICO publishes templates. The request is normally free.
  • Timing: one calendar month, extendable by up to two months for complex requests if they tell you why. If they ask for proof of identity, the month starts when they receive it.
  • If they do not act: complain to the company first, then to the ICO.

Sources: ICO: The right to object to the use of your data, ICO: Your right to get your data deleted.

European Union

7. Erasure and objection under the GDPR

  • Deletion: you can ask a company to delete your data when it is no longer needed or was used unlawfully. The right is not absolute; for example, a bank may have to keep records for a legal period. A company that made your data public must take reasonable steps to tell others processing it about your request.
  • Direct marketing: once you object, a company may no longer use your data for direct marketing, and it must tell you that you can object free of charge.
  • How and when: contact the company, or its Data Protection Officer if it has one. It must respond without undue delay and at the latest within one month.

Sources: European Commission: Information for individuals, European Commission: Can I ask a company to delete my personal data?, European Commission: What happens if someone objects to processing.

8. If a company ignores you: your national authority

  • The European Data Protection Board does not handle individual complaints. Complain to the data protection authority in the country where you live, work, or where the breach happened.
  • The EDPB says an authority should deal with a complaint within three months; if it does not, you can go to the courts in its country.

Sources: EDPB: Contact, EDPB: list of national authorities, EDPB: Steps individuals can take.

What removal can and cannot do

  • Removing a listing does not remove the public record or purchase data behind it, which is why profiles come back. Plan to check again every few months.
  • Paid removal services send these same requests for you. They save time but cannot reach every source; we have not tested any of them.
  • Reduce what feeds new profiles: use aliases, protect your phone number, and strip location from photos with the photo metadata remover.

What we have not tested

We have not yet submitted these requests ourselves, so timings and limits come from each organisation's own page, not our observation. Other people-search sites, and other US state registries, are not covered yet. If a link here is broken or a process has changed, email us and we will correct it.
