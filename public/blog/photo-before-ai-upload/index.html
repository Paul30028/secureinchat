What a photo reveals when you upload it to an AI chatbot, and how to remove it first

Phone photos usually carry hidden EXIF data such as GPS position, device and time. What it contains, what removal does and does not cover, and a step-by-step way to clean a photo in your browser before you upload it.

By Paul2026-10-20

When you send a photo to an AI assistant, you send the picture and, unless something removed it, the hidden data stored in the file. We do not claim what a given provider keeps or ignores; that varies and changes. What you can control is what the file contains.

What is hidden in a phone photo

Phone and camera JPEGs usually carry EXIF data: the device make and model, the date and time, often GPS coordinates, and sometimes the software, lens or a serial number. You can see it with our photo metadata remover, which reads the file in your browser and never uploads it.

How to clean a photo before you upload it

  1. Open the photo metadata remover and choose the photo. It shows the GPS location, device and dates found in a JPEG.
  2. Save the clean copy. The page redraws the image onto a blank canvas and saves that as a new file, then re-reads the new JPEG to check no EXIF is left.
  3. Upload the clean copy instead of the original.

What this does not remove

  • Anything visible in the picture: a street sign, a house number, a face, a landmark, a document on a desk, or a screen with text on it.
  • Formats it cannot read: HEIC and animated images are not supported; JPEG, PNG and WebP are.
  • A small quality loss is possible when a JPEG is re-encoded, and the colour profile is dropped.

So before you upload, also look at the picture itself: crop or cover anything that identifies where you are or who is in it. Related: text redactor for the text you paste next to it.
