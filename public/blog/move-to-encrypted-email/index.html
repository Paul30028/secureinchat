Moving to encrypted email: what it protects, what it does not, and how to switch without losing mail

By Paul · 2026-10-22

An encrypted email service stores your mailbox so the provider cannot read it, and encrypts messages end to end when both sides use it. That is a real improvement over a provider that can read every message. It is not magic: email to and from ordinary addresses still passes through the other provider in a readable form. This guide explains the difference, then walks through a move that does not lose mail or lock you out of accounts. Every fact about a provider links to its own documentation. The practical point: if most people who write to you use Gmail or Outlook, their copy of each message stays readable by their provider whatever you use. The encrypted mailbox protects what is stored on your side. Proton suggests keeping subject lines generic for sensitive messages, because subjects are not end-to-end encrypted.

Before you start

  • Secure the new account first. Turn on two-step verification and save its recovery codes offline. Our account checkup lists the steps. An encrypted mailbox you cannot get back into is worse than none.
  • Keep the old account. Do not delete it for months. Some accounts will still send resets there, and the address could otherwise be lost.

The move, step by step

  1. Copy your old mail. Proton's Easy Switch imports from any provider that supports IMAP, with separate guides for Gmail, Outlook and Yahoo. It runs one import per account at a time. Check the current plan limits on Proton's page before you rely on it.
  2. Forward new mail. In Gmail on a computer: Settings, See all settings, Forwarding and POP/IMAP, Add a forwarding address, click the confirmation link sent to the new address, then choose "Forward a copy of incoming mail to". Google recommends keeping Gmail's copy in the inbox. The forwarded copy reaches your new mailbox, but the original still sits in Gmail, readable by Google.
  3. Change the address on your accounts, most important first. Your bank, phone carrier, Apple, Google or Microsoft account, password manager and government services first; shopping and newsletters last. For each, update the login email and the recovery email, and confirm a reset email arrives at the new address.
  4. Tell people. Give the new address to the people you write to. Mail between two users of the same encrypted service is the part that gets end-to-end protection.
  5. Watch the old inbox. Every message that still arrives there through forwarding shows an account you have not moved yet. Move it, then unsubscribe or delete.
  6. After a few months, stop forwarding, and decide whether to keep the old account dormant (with a strong password and two-step verification) or close it. Keeping it dormant protects you if a forgotten account still sends resets there.

Sources: How to import emails with Easy Switch, Automatically forward Gmail messages to another account.

What we have not tested
